Pictures, a story, and an exact answer

1. A strip of small grids

Three to eight frames that show something happening. One frame is hidden.

2. A short story

It doesn’t describe the grids. It helps only if you read it as being about the shapes.

3. Rebuild the hidden grid

Exactly, every cell the right color. Close doesn’t count.

Part 4 · How Narrate works

You see the answer. The model doesn’t.

→ → →
1

You see every grid, including the one the model won’t see.

2

You write a story about what happens. Don’t spell out cells, numbers or coordinates.

3

The model gets the other grids and your story, and has to rebuild the hidden one exactly. It’s qwen3.8-27b, on MindRouter.

4

You see whether it worked. Revise and try again.

Narrate turns the puzzle around. You see all the grids, including the one the model won't see. You know the answer. Your job is to write a story so the model can rebuild the hidden grid from the other grids and your story. You can't spell the answer out cell by cell, or with numbers or coordinates. It has to be a story. The model is qwen3.8-27b, the highlighted row in every table you've seen, running on MindRouter. You press a button, the model tries, and you see whether it worked. Then you revise. This is close to a lot of real AI use. You know what the right answer looks like. The question is how to describe the situation so the tool gets there.

Two stories from the Faculty AI Bootcamp this summer. The gym. Nobody brings a forklift to the gym. Not because it's against the rules, but because the weight is the point. Let the forklift move the bar and something impressive happened, but not to you. But you drove to the gym, and nobody calls that cheating. That's the commute. So some friction in our work is reps, the part that builds the skill: drafting your own argument, working a proof, sitting with a hard reading. Some is commute: formatting citations, converting files. Hand off the commute and keep the reps. The line moves, though. Grammar is commute in a lab report and reps in a writing seminar. The treadmill came from the bootcamp faculty, in their minute essays. AI saves you an hour, and the hour fills up with more output. You run faster and end up where you started.
